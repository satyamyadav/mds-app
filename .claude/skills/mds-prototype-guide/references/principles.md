# Product principles

Every screen should help close the gap between information and action. Data without a clear path to a decision is noise. Ask: what does this person need to do next, and what do they need to know to do it well?

1. **Action over information.** Organise screens by what the user needs to do, not by what the system knows. Reveal detail when it serves a decision, never by default.

2. **The right context, at the right moment.** Show only what is relevant to this point in the workflow — except where hiding information could cause harm. Context decides what appears; consequence decides what persists.

3. **One concept, one name.** Every concept exists once, with one definition and one label. No synonyms that quietly point to different things.

4. **Speak the user's language.** Internal or system vocabulary never reaches the screen. Labels, units and phrasing fit the audience.

5. **Confirmation proportional to consequence.** Routine actions need no friction. Broad, irreversible, or shared-impact actions earn explicit confirmation. Don't cry wolf.

6. **The most consequential information carries the most weight.** Hierarchy follows consequence, not data structure. Status and risk before detail. Actions before records.

7. **Generated and designed look the same.** AI-generated content meets the same standard as hand-built content — same hierarchy, language and visual logic. Where AI output informs a clinical decision, show its provenance, review state, or confidence.

8. **Honest about uncertainty.** When the system doesn't know, say so plainly. Never imply certainty through silence, vague language, or hidden gaps.
